\chapter{Usability Testing Documentation}
\label{app:usability-testing}

This appendix brings together the main details recorded during the two Omnisound usability tests. It provides an overview of the tasks, individual completion times, navigation routes and observations discussed in Chapters 3 and 5.

\section{Test Setup and Tasks}

Six students aged 20--26 participated in the evaluations. Three students (P1--P3) tested the first prototype, V1, and three different students (P4--P6) tested the updated prototype, V2. Both versions were tested using a laptop and web browser.

Yacoub Dawli led the sessions and explained the tasks. Obid Alrhman Aljabi recorded participant behaviour and comments, while Sarshar Chavoshi Rad documented completion times, clicks and navigation routes.

Participants were asked about their usual audio applications and listening habits before using the interface. During the tasks, they were encouraged to explain what they were looking for and what they expected to happen.

The same three tasks were used in both testing rounds.

\subsection*{Finding and Saving}

P1 used Search in the sidebar, P2 used the top search field and P3 found Weezer through Music. All three saved Weezer and located it again in Library.

P1 briefly hesitated when looking for Save beside Play and later suggested making Save more noticeable. The confirmation message helped participants understand where the saved item could be found.

\subsection*{Resuming an Audiobook}

P1 selected the unfinished audiobook directly through Continue Listening. P2 opened Audiobooks, while P3 looked in Library. Both P2 and P3 later explained that they had not noticed Continue Listening.

The audiobook resumed at its existing position for all three participants. The task was completed successfully, although the routes differed in length.

\subsection*{Browsing Podcasts}

P1 and P2 selected Podcasts directly. P3 initially opened Music, noticed the selection and then moved to Podcasts without help.

The main issues identified in V1 were the visibility of Continue Listening and the prominence of Save. These observations helped the group decide what to change in V2.

\subsection*{Finding and Saving}

P4 used the top search field, P5 used Search in the sidebar and P6 found Weezer through Music. All three saved the item and confirmed that it appeared in Library.

P4 noticed Save immediately. P5 briefly looked between Play and Save before selecting it, while P6 saved without recorded hesitation. Participants recognised the Saved state after using the control.

\subsection*{Resuming an Audiobook}

All three participants selected the unfinished audiobook through Continue Listening. Each reached the player at the existing listening position.

The notes recorded two clicks for the resume task for each V2 participant. No hesitation was recorded during this task.

\subsection*{Browsing Podcasts}

All three participants selected Podcasts directly from the sidebar. No incorrect category selections were recorded.

The post-test comments were generally positive about the navigation, saving control and Continue Listening section. Participants also suggested possible future additions, including Recently Played and Library sorting.

\section{Comparison of the Two Rounds}

All nine task attempts were completed in each round without direct assistance. The recorded differences concerned task times, navigation choices and hesitation rather than additional completed tasks.

\begin{table}[!htbp]
\centering
\caption{Average completion times in seconds.}
\label{tab:appendix-times}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.48\textwidth}rr@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Task} & \textbf{V1} & \textbf{V2} \\
\hline
Find and save Weezer & 27.7 & 22.3 \\
Resume unfinished audiobook & 27.0 & 13.3 \\
Browse podcasts & 5.3 & 3.7 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The largest difference was in audiobook resumption. In V1, one of three participants used Continue Listening directly. In V2, all three participants used it.

After V1, the group reduced the Home banner, made Continue Listening more noticeable and strengthened the visual distinction between Save and Saved. The V2 observations showed more direct use of Continue Listening and shorter recorded task times.

The comparison has some limitations. Each prototype was tested by only three participants, and the two rounds involved different people. This means that differences in familiarity and individual behaviour may also have affected the results.

The tasks also covered only a small part of Omnisound. The results describe the tested workflows rather than every possible interaction with the application.

\section{Initial Design Alternatives}
\label{sec:design-alternatives}

The early concept considered separate audio categories and a mixed feed
with filters. Categories give listeners a clear starting point when they
already know which audio type they want. A mixed feed supports browsing
different types together, but needs clear labels and filters to distinguish
them.

Cards and lists offered another choice. Cards leave room for artwork and
visual recognition, but fewer items fit on the screen. Lists show titles,
creators and other information more compactly. Their suitability depends
on whether the user is browsing or looking for a particular item.

The chosen interface combines these ideas: cards in separate Home sections,
a list of search results and one Library with content filters. Each audio
category has a browsing route, while saving follows a shared pattern.
This gave us a starting design that we could test and improve.

\section{Lo-Fi Sketches}
\label{sec:design-sketches}

Figure~\ref{fig:design-sketches} presents the group's three original pencil

entries for audio categories, playlists, options, Library and a store. The
area on the right contains rough content blocks and playback symbols.
Sketch~(b) concentrates on browsing: rows labelled music, podcasts and books
contain small drawings of covers. Sketch~(c) explores a player around
Weezer, with central artwork, a large play symbol, neighbouring controls and
a horizontal volume control below.

These drawings made the layout ideas visible before the interactive screens
were built. They helped the group consider how much space navigation,
content and playback would need. Their rough appearance also left room to
change the arrangement without first producing detailed visual designs.
They were starting points rather than specifications: for example, the
store shown in sketch~(a) was not included in the tested prototype.

\section{Omnisound Interface Structure}
\label{sec:design-structure}

Omnisound has five main screen types: Home, Search, Library, content detail
and the full player. Persistent navigation provides Home, Search and
Library, followed by separate Music, Podcasts and Audiobooks routes. These
remain available when opening content or playback.

Home includes Continue Listening for unfinished spoken audio. Search covers
the sample catalogue, with type filters. Library collects saved items and
offers similar filters. Details provide Save and Play or Resume. The full
player groups listening controls; a smaller player remains available during
browsing after playback opens.

Figure~\ref{fig:design-search-library} shows Weezer in Search and, after
saving, in Library. Results use lists for scanning, while Library uses covers
and titles for recognition. Shared destinations and controls connect the
audio types without requiring different saving and playback patterns.

\section{Hi-Fi Prototype V1}
\label{sec:design-v1}

V1 developed the sketches into the first interactive high-fidelity
prototype. Instead of viewing isolated drawings, users could follow the
main activities from one screen to another. The category rows and artwork
remained part of the design, while labelled buttons and visible states made
searching, saving and resuming available for testing.

The Home screen in Figure~\ref{fig:design-v1-home} starts with a large
introductory banner. Below it, a small Continue Listening label sits above
the heading ``Back in progress''. The unfinished items show their audio
type, title, creator and listening progress. Separate Music, Podcasts and
Audiobooks sections follow further down the page. The sidebar provides a
direct route to each category even when its Home section is below the
visible area.

  large introductory banner and unfinished content. The capture shows the
  upper part of Home; further category rows continue below.}
  \label{fig:design-v1-home}

One important workflow begins by searching for Weezer and opening the
result. Its detail screen places Save beside Play, as shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:design-v1-workflows}(a). Selecting Save changes the label to
Saved and briefly displays ``Weezer saved to your Library''. Opening Library
then shows the saved item, as in Figure~\ref{fig:design-search-library}(b).
The connection matters because saving should provide a route back to the
content, rather than only changing a button on the current screen.

\section{Design Decisions and HCI Connections}
\label{sec:design-hci}

The principles in Section~\ref{sec:interface-principles} guided concrete
parts of the interface. The sidebar labels and categories in
Figure~\ref{fig:design-v1-home} support visibility. Labels such as Music
and Audiobook explain what an item
is before it is opened, without relying only on artwork or colour.

Feedback connects actions to outcomes. Saving changes the label and displays
a confirmation naming Library. Navigation highlights show the current
destination, while play/pause controls reflect the player state. However,
clear feedback after saving does not ensure that Save is easy to find
beforehand, as the first test showed.

Consistency links the audio types. Their cards open similarly arranged
detail pages, with Save beside Play or Resume. The lists below contain
tracks, episodes or chapters as appropriate, while the main interaction
pattern stays familiar. This connects expectations and behaviour as discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:cognition-mental-models}.

Recognition also shaped the screens. Titles and covers help users identify
Library items, and progress bars identify unfinished audio. These cues
reduce the need to remember exact titles or listening positions. Buttons such as Play, Resume and Save use familiar icons and clear labels
to show users what they do. Play and Resume state what will
happen; the heart with Save gives the icon a written meaning. The redesign
addressed cues that needed more emphasis.

\section{Redesign Decisions}
\label{sec:design-redesign}

The first usability test showed that some participants looked for unfinished
audio through Audiobooks or Library instead of Continue Listening. Those
routes worked, but the intended Home shortcut was being overlooked. The
group therefore focused the redesign on making that section easier to
notice, reducing the space taken by the introduction and clarifying the
saving control.

Figure~\ref{fig:design-home-comparison} shows the Home changes. V2 reduces
the banner's height and the space before unfinished content. It also
replaces the small Continue Listening label and ``Back in progress'' heading
with a single, larger Continue Listening heading and a lime marker. The
unfinished cards and their progress information remain. The intention was
to bring attention to content that users could return to, while keeping a
short introduction to Omnisound.

  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/v1/home-comparison.png}\par

  {\small\textbf{(b) V2 Home}\par}
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/v2/home-comparison.png}
  \caption{Home before and after the redesign. Both prototypes were
  captured at a $1440\times900$ viewport and 100\% zoom, then cropped to

  revised heading bring unfinished content higher on V2 Home.}
  \label{fig:design-home-comparison}

The Save control received a smaller change. The first test recorded a brief
hesitation in locating it beside Play, so V2 strengthened its border and
gave the unsaved and saved states more distinct appearances. The label and
confirmation message were retained because they explained the action's
result. Figure~\ref{fig:design-save-comparison} shows the revised states.

The group kept the sidebar, Search, Library and audio categories because
the first evaluation supported these routes. Library also provided another
way to reach unfinished content. We then tested V2 to see whether these changes made the interface easier
to use.

\section{Hi-Fi Prototype V2}
\label{sec:design-v2}

V2 keeps the five main screen types and navigation structure of V1.
The Home comparison in Figure~\ref{fig:design-home-comparison} also shows
the final arrangement: the introduction occupies less space, and the more
direct Continue Listening heading gives unfinished content greater emphasis.
Users can still search, browse categories or open Library through the same
destinations.

On the detail page, the unsaved control uses an outlined heart and the label
Save. After saving, it shows a filled heart, lime styling and Saved. The
comparison in Figure~\ref{fig:design-save-comparison} captures the same
Weezer page before and after selecting Save. The control stays beside Play,
so its position remains familiar while its changed appearance communicates
the current state. The brief Library confirmation is also retained, giving
immediate feedback as well as a state that remains visible on the page.

between the two rounds.
